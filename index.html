Part I

Academic Profile

I am a doctoral student in Ancient Roman History at Fudan University, currently conducting visiting research at the University of Edinburgh (School of History, Classics and Archaeology). My research investigates the mechanisms of imperial legitimacy, political rituals, and monarchical political thought during the Roman Principate.

My broader research program examines the historical evolution of Vota Publica (state religious vows), the imperial cult, metaphors of the body politic, and ancient Greek and Roman ethnic identities—particularly classical discourses of othering and the conceptual boundaries of the "semi-barbarian" (Μειξοβάρβαρος). My methodology integrates classical textual criticism with epigraphy, numismatics, and papyrology.

Research Theses

The Historical Evolution of Vota Publica Rituals in Ancient Rome
MA Dissertation · University of Chinese Academy of Social Sciences · Supervised by Prof. Yujuan Hu
Investigates the transformation of Vota Publica across the monarchy, republic, and imperial periods. Under the Principate, Vota Publica evolved from traditional reciprocal state vows into an essential ritual instrument supporting imperial legitimacy. Linking the emperor's health directly to state security, the Principate reconciled monarchical dominance with republican memory, using decennial and vicennial vow inscriptions on coinage as civic markers akin to republican terms of office.
Images of the Aetolian Ethnic Group and the Definition Standards of Hellenic Identity
BA Thesis · Capital Normal University · Supervised by Prof. Shaoxiang Yan
Analyzes representations of the Aetolians from early "Hellenes" in the Archaic epoch to 5th-century Athenian texts designating them as "non-Hellenes", through to Hellenistic historical narratives. Demonstrates how classical literary representations of peripheral groups reflected changing criteria of pan-Hellenic identity rather than static ethnic realities.
