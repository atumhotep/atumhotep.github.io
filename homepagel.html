            <div class="section-header">

                <span class="section-numeral">Part II</span>

                <h2 class="section-title">Theses & Core Research</h2>

            </div>

 

            <div class="card">

                <div class="entry-title">The Historical Evolution of Vota Publica Rituals in Ancient Rome</div>

                <div class="entry-meta">MA Dissertation · University of Chinese Academy of Social Sciences · Supervised by Prof. Yujuan Hu</div>

                <div class="entry-desc">

                    This dissertation examines the historical evolution of <em>Vota Publica</em> rituals across the monarchy, republic, and imperial periods. Under the Principate, <em>Vota Publica</em> transitioned from reciprocal state vows for collective welfare into a paramount ceremonial instrument for imperial legitimacy. By linking the emperor's health and safety directly to the preservation of the <em>res publica</em>, Roman emperors reconciled the tension between monarchical reality and Republican facade, utilizing imperial vows and coin titulature (marking quinquennial and decennial vows) as a quasi-coronation ceremony.

                </div>

            </div>

 

            <div class="card">

                <div class="entry-title">Images of the Aetolian Ethnic Group and the Evolution of Hellenic Identity</div>

                <div class="entry-meta">BA Thesis · Capital Normal University · Supervised by Prof. Shaoxiang Yan</div>

                <div class="entry-desc">

                    Analyzes the shifting representations of the Aetolians from early "Hellenes" in the Archaic epoch to 5th-century Athenian discourses designating them as "non-Hellenes" and later Hellenistic confrontations. Demonstrates that historical portrayals of peripheral Greek groups reflected changing Athenian and pan-Hellenic identity standards rather than an unchanging ethnic reality.

                </div>

            </div>
