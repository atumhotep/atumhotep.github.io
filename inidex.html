Part I

Academic Profile

I am a doctoral researcher in Ancient Roman History at Fudan University, currently conducting research as an academic visiting student at the University of Edinburgh (School of History, Classics and Archaeology). My doctoral research investigates the mechanisms of imperial legitimacy, political rituals, and monarchical ideology during the Roman Principate.

My broader academic focus spans the historical evolution of Vota Publica (state religious vows), the imperial cult, political metaphors of the body politic, and ancient Greek and Roman ethnic identity construction—particularly the conceptual borders between Hellenes and "barbarians" (Μειξοβάρβαρος). My methodology bridges classical textual critique with non-literary sources, including Roman epigraphy, numismatics, and papyrology.

Part II

Theses & Core Research

The Historical Evolution of Vota Publica Rituals in Ancient Rome
MA Dissertation · University of Chinese Academy of Social Sciences · Supervised by Prof. Yujuan Hu
This dissertation examines the historical evolution of Vota Publica rituals across the monarchy, republic, and imperial periods. Under the Principate, Vota Publica transitioned from reciprocal state vows for collective welfare into a paramount ceremonial instrument for imperial legitimacy. By linking the emperor's health and safety directly to the preservation of the res publica, Roman emperors reconciled the tension between monarchical reality and Republican facade, utilizing imperial vows and coin titulature (marking quinquennial and decennial vows) as a quasi-coronation ceremony.
Images of the Aetolian Ethnic Group and the Evolution of Hellenic Identity
BA Thesis · Capital Normal University · Supervised by Prof. Shaoxiang Yan
Analyzes the shifting representations of the Aetolians from early "Hellenes" in the Archaic epoch to 5th-century Athenian discourses designating them as "non-Hellenes" and later Hellenistic confrontations. Demonstrates that historical portrayals of peripheral Greek groups reflected changing Athenian and pan-Hellenic identity standards rather than an unchanging ethnic reality.

Part IV

Fieldwork, Monuments & Epigraphy

Visual records and monumental inscriptions from historical surveys across the Forum Romanum and Imperial Rome.

Arch of Septimius Severus (Attica Detail)
CIL VI 1033 · IMP CAES LVCIO SEPTIMIO... PROPAGATVM Monumental dedicatory inscription (AD 203) documenting Severan Parthian campaigns and dynastic legitimacy. Noteworthy for the erasure and re-carving of Geta's name following his damnatio memoriae.
Arch of Constantine at Dusk
ARCVS CONSTANTINI · AD 315 Triumphal arch dedicated to Constantine I celebrating the victory over Maxentius at the Battle of the Milvian Bridge, exemplifying late antique imperial ideology and the reuse of Trajanic, Hadrianic, and Aurelian spolia.
Temple of Antoninus and Faustina
DIVO ANTONINO ET DIVAE FAVSTINAE EX S C Colossal cipollino marble colonnade in the Roman Forum, testifying to imperial deification (consecratio) and the religious architecture of the Antonine dynasty.
Statue of a Vestal Virgin
ATRIUM VESTAE · FORVM ROMANVM Memorial statue of a Virgo Vestalis Maxima within the House of the Vestals, embodying traditional Roman civic religion, ritual purity, and the eternal continuity of Rome.
Arch of Titus on the Velia
SENATVS POPVLVSQVE ROMANVS DIVO TITO Constructed by Domitian c. AD 81, depicting the deification of Titus riding an eagle and the triumphal procession following the Jewish War.
Palatine Hill Substructures
PALATIVM · IMPERIAL MONUMENTALITY Massive brick-faced concrete arches supporting the imperial palaces overlooking the Forum Romanum, visual markers of the concentration of administrative authority during the Principate.
